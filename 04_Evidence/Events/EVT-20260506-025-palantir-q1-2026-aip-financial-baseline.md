## Inferences

- The filing places agent tooling and evaluation inside a broader data, ontology and deployment platform rather than presenting AIP as a standalone model product.
- Strong company-wide growth and margins coexist with customer concentration and self-funded deployment effort, so company-level results cannot be assigned to AIP without segment or product disclosure.

## Research judgment

The 10-Q supplies an authoritative company-wide operating baseline and confirms how management defines AIP and Ontology. It does not disclose AIP revenue, Agent usage, deployment conversion or product-level margins; those omissions prevent attributing the reported financial growth to Agent adoption.

## Alternative explanations

- The reported growth may be driven by government contracts or non-Agent platform demand rather than AIP.
- AIP adoption may accelerate customer expansion but require enough deployment support to limit incremental margins.

## Unknowns

- AIP-specific revenue, bookings, customer count, retention and gross margin are not disclosed.
- The conversion rate and cost of pilots or bootcamps are not disclosed.
- The filing does not quantify production Agent tasks or business outcomes.

## Follow-up indicators

- Track AIP-specific commercial metrics in earnings calls and future filings.
- Track customer concentration, professional-services intensity and pilot conversion.
- Seek independent customer evidence separating AIP outcomes from broader Palantir deployments.
