## Inferences

- The production role tested here is bounded autonomy under continued human responsibility, not removal of the human service interface.
- Aggregate speed gains can coexist with worse subjective experience in the tasks delegated to the agent.
- A handoff mechanism alone is insufficient when the system recognizes emotional deterioration too late.

## Research judgment

This is direct counterevidence to a simple interface-replacement or centralized-control-layer narrative. It shows real task execution and efficiency gains, but only within a narrow eligible subset and with material dependence on human supervision, monitoring quality and intervention timing. The evidence does not show that agents cannot become an interface or control layer; it narrows the conditions under which that transition is likely to work.

## Alternative explanations

- Lower ratings may reflect communication style rather than failure to resolve the issue.
- The system was early and limited to less than 10% of chats; later models or better monitoring may improve both coverage and experience.
- Customer service may be unusually sensitive to emotion and may not represent back-office workflows.

## Unknowns

- The paper does not disclose software revenue, deployment cost or long-run labor demand.
- The persistence of the measured effects after further model and workflow iteration is unknown.
- The interface usage displaced by the agent is not measured.

## Follow-up indicators

- Track AI-eligible share, intervention rate and time-to-intervention in later deployments.
- Track customer ratings and retrials separately from speed and containment.
- Seek comparable randomized evidence outside customer service.
