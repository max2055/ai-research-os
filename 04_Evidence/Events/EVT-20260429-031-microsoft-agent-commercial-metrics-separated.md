## Inferences

- Microsoft is operating a mixed commercial model comprising seats plus consumption rather than a single Agent price metric.
- Managed-agent inventory, monthly active usage, credit purchasing, seat count and revenue are different measures and cannot be added or substituted for one another.
- The LinkedIn disclosure is a product-specific financial contribution; the USD 37 billion AI ARR is a company-wide AI measure and cannot be attributed to Agents.

## Research judgment

The call materially improves commercial measurement by separating five layers: paid seats, relative usage intensity, usage-credit adoption, governance inventory and one product-specific revenue run rate. It still does not disclose total Agent runs, total Copilot Credit revenue, Agent 365 revenue or a consolidated Microsoft Agent revenue figure. The evidence supports a seats-plus-consumption transition while leaving overall Agent unit economics unresolved.

## Alternative explanations

- Credit purchasing growth may reflect packaging changes rather than underlying task volume.
- Seat and usage growth may be concentrated in trials, promotions or a small group of heavy users.
- LinkedIn agentic product revenue may not represent the economics of Copilot Studio or Agent 365.

## Unknowns

- Absolute first-party Agent monthly active usage and Agent run counts are not disclosed.
- Copilot Credit revenue, realized price per task and gross margin are not disclosed.
- Agent 365 paid customers, revenue and active managed-agent share are not disclosed.
- A consolidated Microsoft Agent revenue contribution is not disclosed.

## Follow-up indicators

- Track absolute Agent runs and credits consumed, not only growth rates.
- Track Copilot Credit revenue, price realization and gross margin.
- Track Agent 365 paid adoption separately from managed-object inventory.
- Track additional product-specific Agent revenue disclosures.
